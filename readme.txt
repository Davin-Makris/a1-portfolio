A reminder on academic integrity, as described in the syllabus.

In general, the course staff expects that you will look at code and examples from many online resources as part of the assignments, particularly to resolve syntax and understand frameworks. We expect that you'll use other libraries you find, and will even require it in some assignments. These practices are often critical to the work of developers today. The best developers are adept at interpreting the examples they see, customizing them to their specific situation, and citing their sources so they can find them later. We expect you to do the same.

While learning from examples is encouraged, attempting to pass an existing project or example from the web as your own is not allowed. If you ever have a question about what is or is not appropriate, feel free to ask the course staff!

Talking to classmates about class material, assignment requirements, etc. is a great way to verify ideas and get feedback. But this distinctly does *not* permit attempting to pass off someone else’s code as your own. Talking over ideas and approaches is allowed, but the work that you produce and submit must be your own.

3. Did you ignore any of the warnings or errors presented by the accessibility checker? If so, why does 
this not seem like an accessibility concern? If it's useful, you can consolidate your thoughts on multiple warnings/errors if the rationale is similar.

I did not have any errors in the accessibility checker, but I had the following warnings:
Check 1.1: Text Alternatives
- I did not handle this warning because my sole image is decorational, and is largely used for formatting reasons. 
- I am also following the example in class to keep the alt text simple, as the image does not convey anything to the user. 

Check 1.3: Adaptable
- I have two warnings regarding right to left text and direction of text, my text is all in English and horizontal. 
I do not feel I need these indicators. 
- I have two warnings regarding visual lists not being marked and tabular information missing markup. My visual lists are 
all under <ul> tags and I do not have any tables.
- I have a warning about input control being far from label, but the labels are all imediately above the text fields. 

Check 1.4: Distinguishable
- I have three warnings regarding potential lack of contrast in the image, but the image contains no text and no meaning.

Check 2.4: Navigable
- I have a warning on the lack of a skip to content link. I have these links on the top of the nav bar. 
- I have several warnings on h1 and h2 being used for formatting, but I feel I have used them logically with h1 
representing sections and h2 representing subsections. 
- I have a warning on input possibly being used with color alone, but it also has a bounding box for the text field. 
- I have several warnings regarding either the title not being descriptive of the site, the link text not being descriptive 
of the links or unrelated links being grouped together. I have put my best effort to label these links. 
- I have a warning about a lack of a site map. My webpage does not include any other webpages within itself. 

Check 3.1: Readable
- I have a warning regarding text in another language. My webpage content is only in English.

Check 3.2: Predictable
- I have a warning regarding repeated content not being arranged in the same order. I have put my best effort to keep 
consistiency within the sections, but I feel some variability is expected such as having differing numbers of bulletpoints 
per project. 
- I have a warning about tab order, but I have ordered it in the order the sections appear on my portfolio.
- I have a warning regarding long quotations possibly being seperated, but I do not include quotations in my text content.

Check 3.3: Input Assistance
- I have several warnings regarding making required text input clear and lack of error codes providing assistance. I do not 
currently have the contact me section hooked up to a back end so I can not verify if the text entry is proper or not.
